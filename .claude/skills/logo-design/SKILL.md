# Logo Design Guide — Passionate Taskers

Any logo work must feel native to the brand system already locked in, not bolted on: **Craft Premium** — deep warm charcoal (`#17140F`) + copper (`#C87F41`), geometric sans display direction (Futura / Century Gothic / Avenir Next), warm-luxury positioning inspired by Apple-style restraint. The current placeholder is a rounded-square "PT" monogram in copper — functional but generic. Real logo work should clear a higher bar than that box.

## What reads as "premium / luxury" (psychology)

- **Simplicity and symmetry read as trustworthy and higher-quality** (processing fluency) — the brain rewards forms it can parse instantly. Ornate or fussy marks read as try-hard, not premium.
- **Negative space is a feature, not empty space.** Luxury marks rarely use more than one or two colors and give the mark room to breathe. Resist adding detail to "finish" it.
- **Geometric precision underneath.** Even hand-crafted-feeling marks are built on consistent proportions — matched stroke weights, true circles, aligned angles. Precision reads as quality control; sloppiness reads as cheap, no matter the concept.
- **Timeless over trendy.** No gradients, drop shadows, bevels, or glossy effects — these date a mark immediately and read as amateur rather than premium.

## What reads as "warm" specifically (vs. cold luxury — jewelry, finance)

- Slightly rounded terminals and corners over razor-sharp points. Sharp reads clinical/corporate; soft reads human and approachable.
- **Avoid the literal trade-tool cliché** — wrench, hammer, toolbox. Every competitor in this space already uses one; leaning on it undercuts the premium positioning by reading as a hardware-store logo, not a luxury service brand.
- Favor marks suggesting connection or care without being literal — e.g. two forms meeting (customer + tradesperson), an abstract monogram, or one confident geometric shape that becomes ownable through consistent repetition rather than complexity.

## Non-negotiable practical constraints

Tie directly to the accessibility work already done on this project — a logo that fails these isn't done yet:

1. **Works as a single flat color.** Test at 100% black and 100% copper, zero gradients or effects. If it only works in full color, it's not finished.
2. **Legible from 16×16px (favicon) up to a large hero mark.** No fine detail that disappears at small size.
3. **Sufficient contrast on both brand surfaces** — the dark Craft Premium background (`#17140F`) and the light accessible-companion background (`#FAF6F0`). Check both, not just the one it was designed on.

## When the user shows a candidate logo

1. Check it against the established palette — does it use the copper/charcoal system, or introduce colors that fight it?
2. Run the grayscale test and the small-size test.
3. Ask the real positioning question: could this belong to a luxury interior design or architecture firm rather than a trades marketplace? That's the actual bar for "warm luxury," not "does it look nice."
4. Give specific, actionable feedback tied to the principles above — not just "looks good" or "looks off." Name which principle is or isn't being met.
5. Prefer concrete refinements over starting from scratch, when the core concept is sound.
